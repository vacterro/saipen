ESTABLISHED INCIDENT

In the SAITULS project:

- E-3003 created an operator-gated WAIT concerning the Flush(true) physical power-loss durability ceiling.
- E-3009 canonically recorded an OPERATOR_ACCEPTED_LIMITATION decision through a committed SAIOPS ticket_move operation.
- E-3010 and E-3011 subsequently reported that the operator gate had been satisfied.
- STATE.next_action nevertheless retained the original WAIT.
- STATE.phase remained SCOUT and safety_valve_mode remained hard_wait.
- Repeated saipen continue calls returned the same RESTATE_AND_STOP condition.
- The operation record does not establish the human origin of E-3009.
- No operator carrier digest was available in the reported host session.
- T-285 and T-286 remained unexecuted.

CRITICAL DISTINCTION

A committed SAIOPS operation proves that an operation was recorded. It does not independently prove operator authority.

Never promote E-3009 to verified operator authority solely because it is canonical, committed, referenced by checkpoints, or described as an operator decision.

REPORTED ENGINE LOCATIONS

The incident analysis identifies these locations. Verify them against the live SAIPEN source before modifying:

- saipen_engine/state.py: binding_brake and binding_wait
- saipen_engine/router.py: RESTATE_AND_STOP routing
- saipen_engine/operator_task.py: operator-carrier validation
- SOURCES.md: witness taxonomy and authority restrictions
- Recovery commands and SAIOPS decision commit paths

TARGET OUTCOME

A genuinely authenticated operator decision can satisfy its exact gate, and the resulting admissible next action survives checkpoint, restart, resume and reconciliation.

An unauthenticated or merely model-supplied decision cannot satisfy that gate.

PHASE A: ARCHITECTURAL DIAGNOSIS

Trace the complete flow:

operator input
  -> carrier validation
  -> decision scope validation
  -> SAIOPS commit
  -> authoritative state transition
  -> next_action projection
  -> binding brake
  -> router admission
  -> restart and reconcile

Determine whether next_action is stored as stale independent text or is derived from a versioned gate state.

Identify where operator-decision commitment and WAIT invalidation become disconnected.

Check whether the engine already contains a verified authority mechanism suitable for reuse.

Do not implement another independent override layer if the existing authority mechanism can be corrected.

PHASE B: PROVENANCE CONTRACT

Separate:

1. Canonical operation provenance.
2. Authenticated operator-input provenance.
3. Authorization for the exact requested decision.
4. Authority persistence across restart.

An E-3009-style committed operation without verifiable operator-input provenance remains authority-unverified.

The engine must not generate its own digest and then use that digest to authenticate model-supplied instructions.

Where persistent authority receipts are required, ensure they are bound to an independently validated operator carrier, exact decision content, scope, project identity and applicable gate identity.

Verify receipt integrity before reuse.

If the existing architecture cannot establish these guarantees, refuse the transition rather than fabricating proof.

PHASE C: GATE LIFECYCLE

Introduce or repair one authoritative transition that:

- Validates the operator witness.
- Validates decision scope.
- Records the accepted limitation.
- Resolves only the corresponding WAIT.
- Projects a new next_action.
- Persists the transition consistently.
- Supports deterministic recovery after interruption.

The gate must not become satisfied merely because a matching natural-language statement appears in LOG, BOARD or STATE.

Prevent half-committed states in which the decision appears accepted but next_action retains an obsolete requirement.

A reconciler must not resurrect a gate already discharged by a valid, correctly scoped and verifiable decision.

Conversely, it must not automatically discharge the historical E-3009 gate while authority remains unverifiable.
